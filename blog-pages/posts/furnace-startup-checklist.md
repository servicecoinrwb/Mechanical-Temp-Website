That first chilly morning in Metro Detroit has arrived, and your hand is hovering over the thermostat. Before you officially kick off the heating season, running through a quick checklist can prevent common problems, save you money on energy bills, and keep your family safe. Here's what every homeowner should do before turning on the furnace for the first time.

---

## 1. Change Your Air Filter

This is the single most important and easiest task. A dirty, clogged filter restricts airflow, forcing your furnace to work harder than necessary. That wastes energy and can lead to overheating and system damage. Start every season with a fresh filter.

## 2. Test Your Thermostat

Make sure your thermostat is working correctly. Switch it from "cool" to "heat" and set the temperature a few degrees higher than the current room temperature. You should hear the furnace click on within a minute or so. If not, you might have a thermostat issue or something bigger.

## 3. Clear the Area Around Your Furnace

Your furnace needs room to breathe. Over the summer, boxes, storage bins, and other items tend to pile up around it. Keep at least three feet of clear space around the unit to prevent a fire hazard and allow proper airflow.

## 4. Open All Your Vents and Registers

Walk through your home and check that all heating vents are open and not blocked by rugs, furniture, or drapes. Closing vents in unused rooms doesn't save money — it disrupts the pressure balance in your HVAC system and reduces efficiency.

## 5. Listen for Unusual Noises on Start-Up

When you first turn on the heat, a slight burning smell for a few minutes is normal. That's dust burning off the heat exchanger. But listen carefully for loud banging, scraping, or rattling. Those can mean a loose part or a serious mechanical issue that needs attention now.

## The Final Step: Schedule a Professional Tune-Up

The steps above matter, but nothing replaces a full safety and performance tune-up from a qualified technician. A professional inspection catches carbon monoxide leaks, replaces worn parts before they fail, and gets your system running at peak efficiency for the cold months ahead.

Don't wait for a breakdown in the middle of a Michigan winter.
